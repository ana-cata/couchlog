# Couchlog: project plan

_Created 2026-10-27. This is a living document: update it at the end of every week._

## 1. What we are building

Couchlog is a web app to track the movies and series I watch.

- Keep a **watchlist** of what I want to see.
- Register the **movies and episodes I have already seen**.
- See a **calendar of release dates** for everything on my list.

**Who it is for:** me and a few close friends, who sign in from outside the home with only their email and a one-time code. It is a personal project, not commercial, with no public sign-ups. Separately, a **public demo** lets anyone (for example a recruiter) try the app with a throwaway account full of sample titles. It is its own copy on a free hosting service (Render), never on the Pi and never with my data (card #98, section 8).
**Platform:** a web app that runs on my own server at home (self-hosted). I open it in a browser on my computer or phone, and can add it to the phone's home screen (a PWA). Native iOS/Android apps are out of scope.
**Guiding rule:** keep it basic and simple.

What this means in practice:

- **Data belongs to a user from day one.** Even with one user, every saved item is tied to an account, so adding friends later is a small change, not a rewrite.
- **Login stays simple.** At home, my username and password. From outside, an email and a one-time code, with no password for anyone. The allow list on Cloudflare is the only thing I manage. No public sign-up page.
- **No paid services and no commercial features.** This also keeps us inside TMDB's free non-commercial terms (the TMDB credit stays visible). If a feature turns out to need a paid service, it is removed from the plan. One exception: the domain name, about 11 USD a year (decided on card #96).
- **Access in two stages.** Stage 1: the app runs on my home server and I use it only inside my home network. Stage 2: reach it from outside the home at a public address where only emails on my allow list get through, using Cloudflare Tunnel and Cloudflare Access, with the router staying closed. See sections 8 and 9.

## 3. Features

Priority uses MoSCoW: Must, Should, Could, Won't. The first version (the MVP) is the Must list only. Should features come next, then Could.

### How the app is organized

The header has **Search**, **Movies**, **Series**, **Calendar** and **Profile** (plus About), and the Couchlog name goes to Home.

- **Movies** has two tabs: **To Watch** (films I want to see) and **History** (films I have watched).
- **Series** has four tabs, in this order, and the **Series** link in the header opens the first:
  - **Watching**: at least one episode watched, and not finished. A series I have caught up on that is still running stays here (it only leaves Up Next until a new episode airs), and so does a series that has ended but that I have not watched completely.
  - **To Watch**: on my list, with no episode watched yet.
  - **Finished**: every aired episode watched **and** TMDB says the series has ended or been cancelled. Both are needed.
  - **Dropped**: a small list of the series I gave up on (feature 12). It is the only tab I set by hand, and it wins over the other three.
- To Watch, Watching and Finished are worked out from my watches and TMDB's series status. Marking the first episode moves a series from To Watch to Watching, and un-marking it moves it back by itself.
- In the **public demo** (card #98, `docs/demo.md`) the pages are the same, with a banner on every page saying it is a demo, and the login page is only a **Try the demo** button.

### Must: the first version

**1. Login and time zone.** Username and password (Django's default login). I create the accounts myself in Django's admin page, with no public sign-up page. Every saved item belongs to an account. Each account also has a time zone, chosen directly from a list at the first login (the browser's time zone is suggested) and changeable later. A **Profile page** shows my account and my time zone (where I can change it) and has the log out button; my statistics are added to it later (feature 9). The time zone decides what "today" means for that user, so "today", "tomorrow" and "in 3 days" are right (features 10 and 11). The country used for streaming services (feature 14) is taken from the time zone (for example Europe/Lisbon means Portugal). The time zone also turns a movie's release date into the user's own day (feature 7).
*Done when:* I can log in and out, the pages that need a login are closed to everyone else, my time zone is saved with my account, and I can see and change it on my Profile page.

**2. Search movies and series.** A search box that asks TMDB and shows posters, titles and years. Search results are not stored; only what I add is saved.
*Done when:* typing a title shows matching movies and series, and I can open one.

**3. To Watch lists.** The titles I want to see, in two lists: Movies → To Watch and Series → To Watch. I can add or remove a title from the search results or from its own page.
*Done when:* I add a title, reload the page and still see it, I can remove it, and I can undo the removal.

**4. Mark a movie as watched.** One button on the movie page. It saves the date (today by default, and I can change it). A watched movie leaves the To Watch list and goes into Movies → History.
*Done when:* marking a movie moves it to my history with a date, and I can undo it.

**5. Mark episodes as watched.** On the series page, each season lists its episodes with checkboxes. Three ways to mark: one episode; a whole season; or "watched up to here", which marks every episode up to the chosen one. "Up to here" has no button of its own: the round check button asks for it, with a pop-up "did you watch the earlier episodes too?" whenever the episode I press skips episodes I have not marked (the ones that have aired between the nearest episode I marked before it and it, or everything before it when nothing before it is marked); "Yes" marks the skipped ones and the pressed episode, "No, only this one" marks just it (changed on card #35; before, it was asked only on the first mark in a series). The season button turns into "mark the season as not watched" once the season is complete, and pressing it again is how a whole season is undone (no notice and no Undo button). Episodes that have not aired yet cannot be marked. Because I cannot import my old history, this is how I will enter it, so "up to here" must be quick. Episodes have no date box: each watch saves the day I pressed the button (in my time zone), and only movies let me choose the date or leave it "unknown". Marking an episode moves the series from To Watch to Watching (see "How the app is organized").
*Done when:* each of the three ways saves the right episodes, and each can be undone: one episode by the small "−" next to its green circle (since card #43; pressing the green circle again counts a rewatch), a whole season in one step by pressing its button again. ("Up to here" has no one-step undo of its own, unless it marked a whole season; decided on review.)

**6. Up Next with progress bars.** The home screen shows, for each series in the Watching tab, the next episode I have not seen (only episodes that have already aired). A dropped series is not shown. Checking it off marks it watched and the next one appears. Each series and each season shows a progress bar (watched episodes out of aired episodes).
*Done when:* after I mark an episode, the next one appears and the bar moves; a series with nothing left to watch leaves the list until a new episode airs; and undoing a check-off brings the episode and the bar back.

**7. Calendar of upcoming releases.** A calendar with the release dates of the episodes and movies I am waiting for: the series I am watching and the titles on my To Watch list. A daily task collects the dates from TMDB and saves them; the calendar screen only reads what is saved. Only the day is shown, never the hour. Episode dates are used exactly as TMDB gives them, with no conversion between time zones, so a day may occasionally be off by one for a show from another country. Movies are on the earliest cinema release date and the earliest streaming (digital) release date TMDB lists in any country, each turned into a day in the user's time zone (decided on card #12; no country is looked up), so a film that is not out yet shows as soon as TMDB has a cinema date. TMDB often lists only the first episode of a new season, so the weekly dates of the rest come from TVmaze (decided on card #33); TMDB wins when both have an episode. The calendar only needs to give an idea, so an approximate date is fine.
*Done when:* an upcoming episode of a series I am watching or have on my To Watch list appears on the air date TMDB gives, and removing the series removes it from the calendar.

### Should: right after the first version

**8. Rewatch.** A movie that is marked as watched gets a "Watch again" button; each press saves another watch with its own date (the same date box as the first mark), the movie's page lists its watches, and each can be removed on its own. For an episode there is no button: pressing its round check button again counts as another watch (the row shows "2×"), and a small "−" next to it takes back the latest watch. A season that is watched through gets "Watch Season N again", which adds one watch to every episode that has aired, and a small "−" next to it takes one rewatch back. A rewatch does not change progress bars, Up Next, the Series tabs or the Calendar, but it counts in the time spent (feature 9). (Changed on 2026-10-30: the button is on movies and seasons only, and for an episode the circle pressed again is the rewatch.)
*Done when:* pressing "Watch again" on a movie adds a new dated watch each time and the movie's page lists them all (History shows the movie once); pressing an episode's circle twice shows it watched twice; "Watch Season N again" adds one more watch to each aired episode; progress bars and Up Next stay the same; taking back a watch removes only that watch. (That time spent grows with each of them is part of feature 9.)

**9. Time spent watching.** A statistic with the total time I have spent watching, shown on the Profile page as years, months, days, hours and minutes (units that are zero are left out: a 100-minute film is "1 hour 40 minutes"), from TMDB runtimes and split into movies and series, with a total. Every watch counts, including rewatches. When an episode has no runtime, the series' typical episode runtime is used: the average of its episodes' runtimes (TMDB's own "typical runtime" field turned out to be empty). The total only covers what I register in Couchlog, so it starts at zero.
*Done when:* marking a 100-minute movie adds 100 minutes, a rewatch adds another 100, and undoing a watch takes its minutes off again.

**10. News section on the home page ("coming soon").** A "News" section on the home page shows what is coming soon from my list. For each series or movie that comes out in less than a week (7 days, one setting I can change), it shows only the banner image and the days left until the release ("today", "tomorrow", "in 3 days"). Nothing else: no text and no details. It replaces push notifications. Days are counted in my own time, using my time zone (feature 1).
*Done when:* an episode airing in 3 days appears with its banner and "in 3 days", and one airing in 10 days does not.

**11. Countdown in days.** The "today", "tomorrow" or "in 3 days" label on each banner of the News section (feature 10). The label is built once, in one function. It is only on the home page: the calendar does not show it (decided 2026-10-30, card #38).
*Done when:* the label is correct, including around midnight and across time zones.

**12. Dropped series.** A "Stop watching" button on a series' page moves it to Series → Dropped, the small list of series I gave up on. Watching and Finished are already worked out by the app (see "How the app is organized"), so this is the only status I set by hand. A dropped series is not in Up Next or the calendar, and its history stays.
*Done when:* dropping a series removes it from Up Next and from Watching but keeps its history, it shows in the Dropped list, and bringing it back puts it in the right tab again.

**13. Ratings and notes.** A star rating (1 to 5 whole stars) and a short note (up to 500 characters) for each movie or series, shown on its page. One Save button saves both; "No rating" removes the stars and an empty note removes the note, each without touching the other. It works for any title, watched or not, and each account sees only its own. What is saved shows as text, and the form opens from an edit icon (a pencil over a square) that is always there, so there is no Undo button and no time limit: editing again is the way back (decided on review of card #40, 2026-11-01; the first version had a 10-second Undo like To Watch). Not included: rating single episodes or seasons, and showing ratings on tiles or lists.
*Done when:* both are saved and shown on the title's page, I can change or remove either one with the edit icon, and I can put them back by editing again.

**14. Where to watch.** On a title's page, the streaming services that have it in my country (taken from my time zone, feature 1), from TMDB. TMDB's API is free for non-commercial use. This data probably needs a JustWatch credit, which I check before building it. If it turns out to need a paid service, this feature is removed from the plan.
*Done when:* the page shows the services for my country, or says none were found.

### Design rules

1. **Every watch is saved as its own entry with a date.** For a movie I choose the date, and it can be "unknown"; for an episode the date is the day I pressed the button, in my time zone. This is what makes Rewatch and Time spent possible.
2. **An episode counts as watched in progress if it has at least one watch entry.** Rewatches only add time.
3. **The 7-day News window is one setting**, not repeated in several places.
4. **The TMDB credit is shown properly:** the approved TMDB logo and the notice "This product uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB." on an About page, linked from the header next to Search and Profile. The logo is less prominent than my own app's name.
5. **Everything can be undone.** Any action that changes my data can be reversed, because I can make a mistake: marking a movie, an episode, a season or "up to here", a rewatch, adding or removing a title on the To Watch list, dropping a series, a rating or a note. Undo is available right after the action and also later (for example by un-marking an episode). A whole season is undone in one step, by pressing its button again; "up to here" is undone one episode at a time (decided on review of card #25). Undo is part of every feature's "done when".
6. **Details from TMDB are saved in my own database** when I add a title (title, poster, episodes, runtimes, dates) and refreshed now and then. The app works from the saved data. Only searching and adding new titles needs TMDB to be up; if TMDB does not answer, I see a friendly message and try again later.

## 4. How we work

I build and test on my **Windows PC**. The **Raspberry Pi** runs the real app with my real data (since M6). Only what is merged into `main` goes to the Pi.

For every feature, repeat this loop. A feature is done when it is tested, merged into `main` and running on the Pi.

1. Take the next card on the GitHub project board (To do, Doing, Done). Each card is one feature from section 3, and its "done when" is the test.
2. Build it on a separate branch (`feature/<name>`), never directly on `main`.
3. Write the tests, and run the app on the PC (`uv run python manage.py runserver`) to try it. To check it on my phone, run it in Docker on the PC (`docker buildx bake --allow=network.host -f docker-compose.yml couchlog`, then `docker compose up -d`) and open `http://<PC address>:3000` from the phone while both are on the home Wi-Fi; the PC's address must be in `DJANGO_ALLOWED_HOSTS` in `.env` (see `docs/deploying.md`).
4. Push the branch and open a pull request. GitHub Actions runs the checks (lint and tests) automatically.
5. Read the changes in the pull request (I do the PR for now). Merge into `main` only when the checks are green.
6. Check the "done when" conditions once more on `main` on the PC.
7. Deploy it to the Pi (below). Move the card to Done and add a line to the status log (section 10).

**Deploying to the Pi** (the full steps, and how to go back, are in `docs/deploying.md`):

1. If the change alters the tables, first make a copy of the database by hand (`backup_db`).
2. On the Pi, pull `main`, then build and start the containers (`docker buildx bake --allow=network.host -f docker-compose.yml couchlog`, then `docker compose up -d`).
3. Do the five-minute check (section 7) from my phone.

The data on the PC stays test data. It never goes to the Pi.

**Rules:**
- **`main` always works.** If a deploy breaks something, go back to the previous commit and rebuild.
- **Database changes** are written as migration files (scripted changes to the table structure) kept in the repo, so the PC and the Pi stay in step. Copy the database file before deploying a change that alters the tables.
- **Secrets** (the TMDB key and the login secret) live in a `.env` file on each machine and never in GitHub.
- **Undo is part of every card.** A feature that changes my data is not done until it can be undone (design rule 5 in section 3).
- **One card at a time**, and keep cards small: if a card takes more than a few days, split it.
- **Who does what:** I decide what to build and when it is done; Claude writes the code and tests and explains what it did.

## 7. Testing approach

| Level | What it checks | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Unit tests | One piece of logic | The "next episode" calculation |
| Integration tests | Logic with the database | Marking a season saves the right episodes |
| End-to-end tests | A full path in a real browser | Log in, search, add a series, tick an episode, see it in the calendar |
| Manual testing | Me, on the PC and on my phone over the home Wi-Fi (on the Pi since M6) | The "done when" checklist from section 3 |

**How tests work:**
- **Made-up TMDB responses.** Tests use saved example answers instead of calling the real TMDB, so they are fast and never fail because TMDB is down.
- **A separate test database.** Tests use their own temporary SQLite database, never the one with my real history.
- **A fixed "today".** Tests that depend on dates (coming soon, countdown, calendar) set the date themselves, so they give the same result every day.
- **Tools.** pytest (with pytest-django) for unit and integration tests, respx for the made-up TMDB answers, time-machine for the fixed date, Playwright for Python for end-to-end tests, and ruff for linting.
- **Where they run.** On my PC with `uv run pytest` while I build, and automatically on GitHub for every pull request (section 4). A pull request is merged only when they pass.
- **Keep it basic and simple.** Test the logic that is easy to get wrong (next episode, progress, marking a season, the day in my time zone, time spent, undo) and one main path through the screens. No need to test every button.

**Undo is tested too:** for every action that changes data, a test does it, undoes it, and checks that the data and the progress are back as they were.

**Edge cases to test early:**
- Time zones: "today" and "tomorrow" follow the user's time zone, not the server's.
- Specials, and episodes without an air date yet.
- Movie release dates differ by country and type (cinema or streaming); the calendar uses the earliest cinema one and the earliest streaming one, and a movie with neither is left out.
- Episodes added or renumbered after a season was already watched.
- A series with no aired episodes yet, and a series where everything is watched (Watching if it is still running, Finished only if TMDB says it ended).
- A rewatch must not change progress, and undo must work after reloading the page.
- Adding the same title twice.
- TMDB slow or not answering: a friendly message, no crash.
- "Today" and "tomorrow" around midnight, in my time zone.
- An episode with no runtime (use the series' typical runtime).

**When each level arrives:**
- Week 2: the automatic checks and the first simple test.
- M1: unit and integration tests for search and the To Watch list, and the first end-to-end test (log in, search, add a title, remove it). Each later milestone extends it.
- M3 and M4: the heaviest logic tests (next episode, seasons, time zones, undo).
- Before each milestone ends: a quick manual check against the real TMDB, to see that its answers still look like my made-up ones.

**After each deploy to the Pi (from M6):** a five-minute manual check: log in, search for a title, mark an episode and undo it, open the calendar. The steps, with what each one should show, are in `docs/deploying.md` under "The five-minute check".

## 8. Going live

"Going live" here means running on the Pi at home. It only starts after the MVP is finished (section 4).

```
My PC (build and test) -> GitHub (code) -> Raspberry Pi (Docker container: the app + a SQLite file)
                                                  |
                                  Daily task (updates the calendar from TMDB)

Stage 1: my phone and PC reach the Pi on the home Wi-Fi
Stage 2: from outside the home, Cloudflare (email allow list) -> tunnel the Pi opened -> the app

Public demo (separate): anyone -> Cloudflare (rate limiting rule) -> Render (its own copy of the app and its own database)
```

### Stage 1: on the home network

- **Two versions of the app:** my PC (development, with a test database) and the Pi (the real one, with my real data). There are no preview links.
- **One container.** The Pi runs the app in one Docker container (a small Python image, with gunicorn serving the app and WhiteNoise serving the CSS). The database file lives in a Docker volume outside the container, so rebuilding the app does not erase my data.
- **Address.** I open `http://<pi-address>:3000` on the home network. I give the Pi a fixed address in my router, so the bookmark on my phone keeps working.
- **Starts by itself.** The container restarts automatically when the Pi restarts, so the app is back without me doing anything.
- **Calendar task.** A daily task updates the calendar from TMDB. It also runs when the Pi starts, so the calendar is correct after the Pi was off. It is the `daily` container (card #80, `docs/deploying.md`).
- **Backups.** An automatic daily copy of the database, made with SQLite's safe backup command (not a plain file copy while the app is writing), keeping the last 14 copies. The copies go to Cloudflare R2, private online storage away from the house (decided at M6). I also make a copy before any deploy that changes the tables, and I try restoring one copy once, to be sure it works. A backup contains my account and history, so it stays private. It holds no keys: those stay in `.env`, and the login sessions are erased from the copy (card #81, `docs/deploying.md`).
- **Power cuts.** The database uses safe SQLite settings, so a power cut loses at most the last action and does not corrupt the file. I shut the Pi down properly instead of unplugging it. If something still breaks, I restore the latest backup. Booting the Pi from an SSD instead of the SD card is an option later.
- **Updates.** Deploying is manual (section 4). I also update the Pi's system now and then.
- **The router stays closed.** No port forwarding, at any stage. Reaching the app from outside is Stage 2: a public address, but only emails on my allow list get through.
- **Cost:** 0 EUR a month, apart from the Pi's electricity and the domain (about 11 USD a year, card #96). Cloudflare has a payment method on file to turn R2 on, but nothing is paid at this size (the free tier is 10 GB). Cloudflare has no hard spending limit for R2, so Couchlog stops uploading at 5 GB and only uses Standard storage (card #87).

### Stage 2: from outside the home

- **Address.** `https://couchlog.ana-cata.com`, through Cloudflare Tunnel and Cloudflare Access (card #96, `docs/deploying.md`). The tunnel is a third container on the Pi and comes back by itself when the Pi restarts. The home address keeps working.
- **Who gets in.** Only emails on my allow list receive a one-time code. Cloudflare checks this before anything reaches the Pi, and the tunnel refuses a request that skipped it.
- **Friends** install nothing. They type their email and the code and are in Couchlog, with no password. The first time, their account is created by itself (card #97, `docs/deploying.md`). `/admin/` only opens from the home address.
- Adding the app to the phone's home screen as a real app (a web manifest, which needs HTTPS) is now possible through this address. It is not built.

### If other people use it

- A short privacy note saying what is stored (watch history, time zone). GDPR applies if anyone is in the EU.
- The TMDB credit stays visible.

### Before making the repo or app public

- Check the name (domain, trademark, app stores).
- TVmaze's data is licensed CC BY-SA, which has a ShareAlike rule. Fine for a private app; before sharing Couchlog or its data publicly, read the licence again and check what it asks of what we pass on. The public demo has TVmaze off, so it does not come up there (card #98).
- **The public demo** (card #98, built): a separate copy on Render's free plan at `https://couchlog-demo.ana-cata.com`, with throwaway accounts, sample titles and limits, away from the Pi and my data. How it works and its limits are in `docs/demo.md`; deploying it and switching it off quickly in `docs/deploying.md`. Next: the security check (#99), then making the repo public (#100).
